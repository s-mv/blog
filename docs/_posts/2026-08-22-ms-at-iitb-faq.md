### **Q:** What is MS by Reseach? How is it different from Mtech?

**A:** MS by Reseach is a programme offered by the CSE deparment at IITB that
focuses on… research, as the name suggests. So if you are naturally inclined
towards research, try for an MS. But to be more precise, here’s a few
differences that I can think of.

| Dimension                     | Mtech (TA) | MS (TA)     | MS (RAP)                    |
| ----------------------------- | ---------- | ----------- | --------------------------- |
| Programme duration            | 2 years    | 1.5-3 years | 3 years                     |
| Number of electives           | 9          | 5           | 5                           |
| Focus on research             | existent   | yes         | focuses on research project |
| Placement opportunities       | ???        | ???         | ???                         |
| Further reseach opportunities | good       | excellent   | excellent                   |

Some more differences:

- You choose your advisor early on in your MS (usually), while in Mtech your MTP
  guide can be chosen much later.
- While MS students have less courses, they have R&D subjects instead. These are
  graded based on the research work you conduct.
- Your MS thesis has 0 credits. It is a passing criterion (among other things).
  Meanwhile in an Mtech, your MTP has credits. This may sound counterintuitive
  so think why it is so.

Something to note: Doing an Mtech does not completely exempt you from research
either. It just pushes the focus on it towards the latter semesters.

### **Q: Placements in MS??? Why the question marks?**

**A:** If you're doing a master's just for placements, MS might not be for you
for very different reasons than the one you might think of. (In fact, if you
want a job, doing a master's is somewhat redundant with some exceptions.)
Anyway, I digress.

You **can** end up with great placements if you're doing an MS @ IITB. However
it is fundamentally a research degree, so the condition is that you must be
sincere and rigorous. The good thing about the programme is that you might end
up doing what you like even after your thesis wherever you get to go. Another
good thing about the MS programme is that if your guide allows you, you can do
an internship. I've been told that that is a gateway to a lot of free coffee and
even a fulfilling job. However, if you're not research-oriented, you might face
obstacles. You might even find the programme frustrating.

**Unsolicited advice time:** If you're planning to go for a master's at all, try
to find out what professors you might want to work with during it beforehand. It
would give you a lot of clarity. Even if your sole aim is placements. The
alternative is managing adapting into a new environment WHILE mailing 10
different professors for guidance. It's just too chaotic, and people end up
regretting things sometimes.

### **Q:** Is MS a good backup for Mtech?

**A:** Not if you had to ask that question in the first place. It would give you
the IIT tag but the way the degrees are structured is very different. If you
don't mean to do an MS (by _research_) then it's better to do an Mtech at some
other IIT. The Mtech programmes in most IITs are awesome, don't get me wrong.
They're just a different direction altogether, that's all.

### **Q:** What is TA and RA/RAP? What is better?

**A:** If you're a TA (Teaching Assistant), you're supposed to help professors
with coursework, evaluation, doubt solving, etc. for 8 hours a week. An RAP
(Research Assistant under a Project) works on a research project (for example:
BharatGen! (pretty cool I guess? (idk))). Usually an RAP's thesis overlaps with
their project work a little, and they have to work more. They also get paid more
but have to stick around for 3 years.

I don't know much about RA since there are no MS RAs (only Mtech RAs) but I'll
update this when I do.

### **Q:** How do I apply for MS @ IITB? How do I get selected?

**A:** You need to qualify for the written test, which in turn is a qualifier
for an interview. There's also a programming test for MS RAP (more to be updated
on it here).

```
GATE cutoff (X) -> written test (Y) and Programming test -> interview (Z)
```

**For MS TA specifically:**

**GATE cutoff:** Usually a GATE score above X = 650 is needed to apply. It may
be more or less, keep an eye out for this.

**Written test:** The written test is divided into three panels (computing
systems, theoretical systems, intelligent systems). Read up on what these are.
You'll find the syallbus and the question paper on IITB's site:  
<https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/admissions/pg/ms>

Here, Y = (70% GATE Score) + (30% written test performance)

**Interview**: The criteria for qualifying for the interview seems to change a
lot. In 2026 it was _Y_ being more than 50% (see above).

Now,

Z = (70% GATE Score) + (30% Interview performance)

This Z determines your position in IITB's MS waitlist. If you've performed well
till this point, then congrats. You're quite likely to get in.

General timeline:

````
You attempt GATE -> You attempt the written and programming tests -> You sit for the interview (2-3 days later) -> You get the offer (generally 4th COAP round or later)```
````

Note: You CANNOT float an MS offer. You either accept it or reject it.

Note 2: You must have 60%+ in your bachelors to be eligible for MS/Mtech (as of
now). Also, IITB doesn't have CFTI admissions, only IIT Btech admissions (if
you're eligible for that route).

### **Q:** How do I prepare for the written test?

### **Q:** What is the interview like? How long is it? What is asked?

It depends on a lot of factors including your interests and the panel(s) you've
applied to. It is better not make no assumptions and be thorough with subjects
that interest you and align with your panel(s). A bit of DSA knowledge also
doesn't hurt, from what I know.

### **Q:** Does work experience, prior research work, etc. help in the process?

These are not requirements but they can help, especially if you're trying for
RAP since your past experience may align well with a project. It's good to have
projects and some practical experience, if not professional.

### **Q:** I've been selected for the programme. What do I do now?

**A:** First of all, congrats! After you're done celebrating, you should try to
find what research group and professors interest you the most and correspond
with them. The world would be a better place if people communicated with
professors or did internships with them during their bachelors and then joined
research. But this is close enough.
